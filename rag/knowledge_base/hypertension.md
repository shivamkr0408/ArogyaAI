# Hypertension

Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is a common chronic condition in which the force of blood against the artery walls remains consistently elevated. It is often referred to as a "silent killer" because it typically does not cause noticeable symptoms, yet significantly increases the risk of serious health problems such as heart disease, stroke, kidney damage, and vision loss. Risk factors include a family history of hypertension, obesity, high sodium intake, physical inactivity, chronic stress, smoking, and excessive alcohol consumption. Primary (essential) hypertension has no identifiable cause, while secondary hypertension may be linked to underlying conditions such as kidney disease or hormonal disorders.

Because hypertension is usually asymptomatic, many individuals are unaware they have it until a routine check-up reveals elevated readings. In some cases, very high blood pressure may cause headaches, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds, but these are not reliable indicators. Regular blood pressure monitoring is essential for early detection, as untreated hypertension can silently damage blood vessels and organs over years.

General self-care guidance includes adopting a heart-healthy diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins while reducing sodium, saturated fat, and processed food intake. Regular aerobic exercise, maintaining a healthy weight, managing stress, limiting alcohol consumption, and quitting smoking are important lifestyle modifications. Monitoring blood pressure at home with a reliable device can help track trends and support discussions with your healthcare provider.

You should see a doctor if your blood pressure readings are consistently above the recommended range, if you experience symptoms such as severe headaches, chest pain, vision changes, or difficulty breathing, or if you have risk factors for cardiovascular disease. Regular medical check-ups are important for assessing overall cardiovascular health and adjusting your management plan as needed.
