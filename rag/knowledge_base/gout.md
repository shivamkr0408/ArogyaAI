# Gout

Gout is a form of inflammatory arthritis caused by the accumulation of uric acid crystals in the joints, leading to sudden and severe episodes of pain, swelling, redness, and tenderness. Uric acid is a waste product formed when the body breaks down purines, which are found in certain foods and are also produced naturally by the body. When uric acid levels become too high, crystals can deposit in joints and surrounding tissues. Risk factors include a diet rich in red meat, shellfish, and alcohol (especially beer), obesity, certain medications, kidney disease, and a family history of gout. The condition most commonly affects the big toe but can occur in other joints as well.

A typical gout attack begins suddenly, often at night, with intense joint pain that is most severe within the first 24 hours. The affected joint becomes red, swollen, warm, and extremely tender, making even light pressure unbearable. Attacks may last days to weeks, and without treatment, recurrent episodes can lead to joint damage and the formation of tophi, which are lumps of uric acid crystals under the skin. Between attacks, individuals may feel normal, but repeated flare-ups can become more frequent and affect more joints over time.

General self-care guidance includes staying well-hydrated, limiting alcohol consumption (especially beer), reducing intake of high-purine foods such as red meat and shellfish, and maintaining a healthy weight. During an attack, resting the affected joint, applying ice packs, and elevating the joint can help reduce pain and swelling. Medications to lower uric acid levels may be prescribed for long-term management to prevent future attacks.

You should see a doctor if you experience sudden, severe joint pain, especially if accompanied by fever, or if gout attacks become more frequent. A healthcare provider can confirm the diagnosis through joint fluid analysis or blood tests and recommend appropriate treatment to manage acute attacks and reduce uric acid levels to prevent complications such as joint damage and kidney stones.
