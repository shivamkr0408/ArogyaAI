# Psoriasis

Psoriasis is a chronic autoimmune skin condition in which the life cycle of skin cells is dramatically accelerated, causing cells to build up rapidly on the skin's surface. This results in thick, silvery scales and itchy, dry, red patches that can be painful and embarrassing. The condition is driven by an overactive immune system and has a strong genetic component, though environmental factors such as stress, skin injuries, infections, and certain medications can trigger or worsen flare-ups. Psoriasis can affect any area of the body and ranges from mild to severe, with some forms presenting as small patches and others covering large areas of skin.

Typical symptoms include well-defined patches of red skin covered with thick, silvery scales, itching or burning sensations, dry and cracked skin that may bleed, stiff and swollen joints (in cases of psoriatic arthritis), and thickened or ridged nails. The most common pattern involves symmetrical patches on the elbows, knees, scalp, and lower back, though any body area may be affected. Symptoms often cycle, with periods of flare-ups followed by remission, and the severity can vary widely from person to person.

General self-care guidance includes keeping the skin moisturized, avoiding known triggers such as stress, skin injury, and certain medications, and using gentle, fragrance-free skincare products. Regular, short exposure to sunlight may help some individuals, but excessive sun exposure can worsen symptoms. Maintaining a healthy weight, eating a balanced diet, and managing stress through relaxation techniques can also support overall well-being.

You should see a doctor if you notice persistent skin changes, if over-the-counter treatments do not provide relief, if psoriasis is widespread or significantly affects your quality of life, or if you develop joint pain or swelling. A dermatologist can confirm the diagnosis and recommend treatments tailored to the severity and type of psoriasis, including topical therapies, phototherapy, or systemic treatments for more severe cases.
