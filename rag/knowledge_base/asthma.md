# Asthma

Asthma is a chronic respiratory condition characterized by inflammation and narrowing of the airways, leading to recurring episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and coughing. The airways become overly sensitive to various triggers, which can include allergens such as dust mites, pollen, mold, and pet dander, as well as irritants like smoke, strong odors, cold air, and respiratory infections. Genetic predisposition and environmental factors both contribute to the development of asthma. The severity of the condition varies widely, from mild intermittent symptoms to persistent, severe attacks that require emergency care.

Typical symptoms often worsen at night or early in the morning and may be triggered by exercise, allergen exposure, or emotional stress. During an asthma episode, the muscles around the airways constrict, the lining swells, and excess mucus is produced, making it difficult to breathe. Between episodes, many individuals with asthma can live relatively normally, though some experience chronic symptoms. The frequency and intensity of attacks can change over time, influenced by seasonal factors, overall health, and exposure to triggers.

General self-care guidance includes identifying and avoiding personal triggers whenever possible. Using air purifiers, controlling indoor humidity, keeping living spaces clean, and avoiding exposure to tobacco smoke can help reduce flare-ups. Regular physical activity is encouraged with appropriate warm-up to prevent exercise-induced symptoms. Following an asthma action plan developed with a healthcare provider helps ensure consistent management, and keeping rescue inhalers accessible is important for handling unexpected attacks.

You should seek immediate medical attention if symptoms worsen despite treatment, if you are unable to speak in full sentences due to breathlessness, if your rescue medication provides little or no relief, or if symptoms escalate rapidly. Regular follow-up with a healthcare provider is essential for monitoring and adjusting your management plan to maintain good control and reduce the risk of complications.
