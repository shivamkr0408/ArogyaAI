# Hemorrhoids (Piles)

Hemorrhoids, commonly known as piles, are swollen blood vessels in the lower rectum or around the anus. They can be internal, located inside the rectum, or external, found under the skin around the anus. Hemorrhoids are a very common condition, affecting a large proportion of adults at some point in their lives. Contributing factors include chronic constipation or diarrhea, straining during bowel movements, prolonged sitting on the toilet, pregnancy, obesity, and a low-fiber diet. The condition can range from mild and intermittent to chronic and significantly uncomfortable.

Typical symptoms include painless bleeding during bowel movements, itching or irritation around the anal area, discomfort or pain when sitting, swelling near the anus, and in the case of external hemorrhoids, a hard, tender lump. Internal hemorrhoids may protrude outside the anus, causing pain and swelling. Symptoms can flare up during periods of constipation or straining and may improve with dietary changes and lifestyle modifications. Chronic hemorrhoids can significantly affect quality of life and may require medical intervention.

General self-care guidance includes increasing dietary fiber intake through fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to soften stool and reduce straining, drinking plenty of water, and engaging in regular physical activity to promote healthy bowel movements. Sitz baths, warm compresses, and over-the-counter topical treatments may provide symptomatic relief. Avoiding prolonged sitting on the toilet and straining during bowel movements can help prevent worsening of symptoms.

You should see a doctor if you experience rectal bleeding, if symptoms persist despite home care, if you notice a painful lump near the anus, or if discomfort significantly impacts your daily activities. A healthcare provider can confirm the diagnosis, rule out other conditions, and recommend appropriate treatment options ranging from lifestyle modifications to medical procedures.
