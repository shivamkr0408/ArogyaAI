# Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

A urinary tract infection (UTI) is an infection in any part of the urinary system, which includes the kidneys, bladder, ureters, and urethra. UTIs are one of the most common infections worldwide and are more prevalent in women than men due to differences in anatomy. The most common cause is bacteria entering the urinary tract through the urethra and multiplying in the bladder. Risk factors include sexual activity, certain types of birth control, urinary retention, kidney stones, diabetes, and a weakened immune system. UTIs can range from mild bladder infections to more serious kidney infections.

Typical symptoms of a lower UTI (bladder infection) include a strong, persistent urge to urinate, a burning sensation during urination, frequent urination of small amounts, cloudy or strong-smelling urine, and pelvic discomfort. If the infection spreads to the kidneys (upper UTI), symptoms may include fever, chills, back or side pain, nausea, and vomiting. The pattern of symptoms can help distinguish between lower and upper tract infections, with upper UTIs requiring more urgent medical attention.

General self-care guidance includes drinking plenty of water to help flush bacteria from the urinary system, urinating frequently and completely, wiping from front to back after using the toilet, and urinating after sexual activity. Avoiding irritants such as caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods may help reduce bladder irritation. Cranberry products have been studied for their potential to reduce the risk of recurrent UTIs, though evidence is mixed.

You should see a doctor if you experience symptoms of a UTI, especially if you have fever, back pain, or nausea, which may indicate a kidney infection. UTIs are diagnosed through urine tests and are typically treated with antibiotics prescribed by a healthcare provider. Prompt treatment is important to prevent the infection from spreading to the kidneys or causing complications.
