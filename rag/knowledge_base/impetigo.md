# Impetigo

Impetigo is a common and highly contagious skin infection that primarily affects infants and young children, though it can occur at any age. It is caused by bacteria, most commonly Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus pyogenes, which enter the skin through cuts, scrapes, or areas of irritation. Impetigo spreads easily through direct contact with an infected person or contaminated items such as towels, clothing, or bedding. The condition is most prevalent in warm, humid environments and in settings where children are in close contact, such as schools and daycare centers.

Typical symptoms include red sores or blisters that quickly rupture, ooze, and form a honey-colored crust. The sores are most commonly found around the nose and mouth but can appear anywhere on the body. Non-bullous impetigo, the most common form, presents as small red spots that develop into blisters and then crust over. Bullous impetigo causes larger, fluid-filled blisters that may be less itchy. The sores are usually not painful but can be itchy, and scratching can spread the infection to other areas of the body or to other people.

General self-care guidance includes gently washing the affected area with soap and water, keeping the sores clean, and covering them with clean bandages to reduce the risk of spreading. Frequent handwashing is essential, and personal items should not be shared with others. Keeping the child's nails short and discouraging scratching can help prevent the infection from spreading. The condition typically responds well to appropriate treatment and heals without scarring when managed properly.

You should see a doctor if you suspect impetigo, especially if the sores are widespread, not improving, or accompanied by fever. A healthcare provider can confirm the diagnosis and may prescribe topical or oral antibiotics to treat the bacterial infection and prevent complications. Early treatment helps speed recovery and reduces the risk of spreading the infection to others.
