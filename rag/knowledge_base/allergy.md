# Allergy

Allergies are immune system reactions to substances that are typically harmless to most people, known as allergens. Common allergens include pollen, dust mites, pet dander, certain foods, insect stings, and medications. When a person with allergies encounters an allergen, their immune system produces antibodies that trigger the release of chemicals like histamine, leading to various symptoms. Allergies can range from mild annoyances to severe, life-threatening reactions.

Typical symptom patterns vary depending on the type of allergy. Respiratory allergies may cause sneezing, runny or stuffy nose, itchy or watery eyes, and wheezing. Food allergies can lead to hives, swelling of the face or throat, digestive issues, and in severe cases, anaphylaxis. Skin allergies often present as rashes, itching, or eczema. Symptoms usually appear quickly after exposure to the allergen and can persist as long as the exposure continues.

General self-care guidance includes identifying and avoiding known allergens whenever possible. Keeping living spaces clean, using air purifiers, and washing bedding regularly can reduce exposure to indoor allergens. For mild symptoms, over-the-counter antihistamines may provide relief. It is helpful to keep a symptom diary to identify triggers and patterns, which can be valuable information for healthcare providers.

You should seek medical attention if symptoms are severe, persistent, or significantly impact your daily life. Immediate emergency care is necessary if you experience difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, rapid heartbeat, or dizziness, as these may indicate anaphylaxis. Allergy testing can help identify specific triggers, and a healthcare provider can recommend long-term management strategies.
