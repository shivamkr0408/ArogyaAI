# Arthritis

Arthritis refers to a group of conditions characterized by inflammation of the joints, causing pain, stiffness, and swelling. The most common types are osteoarthritis, which involves wear and tear of joint cartilage, and rheumatoid arthritis, an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks the joint lining. Other forms include psoriatic arthritis, gout, and juvenile arthritis. Risk factors vary by type but may include age, family history, obesity, joint injuries, and autoimmune predisposition. Arthritis can affect people of all ages and is a leading cause of disability worldwide.

Typical symptoms include joint pain, swelling, stiffness (especially in the morning or after periods of inactivity), reduced range of motion, and warmth around the affected joints. In rheumatoid arthritis, symptoms often affect multiple joints symmetrically, such as both wrists or both knees. Osteoarthritis tends to affect weight-bearing joints like the knees and hips. Symptoms may worsen over time or during flare-ups, and the pattern of symptoms can help distinguish between different types of arthritis.

General self-care guidance includes maintaining a healthy weight to reduce stress on joints, engaging in low-impact exercises such as swimming, walking, or cycling to improve joint flexibility and strength, and using hot or cold compresses to manage pain and inflammation. A balanced diet rich in anti-inflammatory foods, adequate rest, and stress management can also support overall joint health. Assistive devices may help reduce strain on affected joints during daily activities.

You should see a doctor if you experience persistent joint pain, swelling, or stiffness that interferes with daily activities, if joints appear deformed, or if symptoms are accompanied by fever or unexplained weight loss. Early diagnosis and treatment can help manage symptoms, slow disease progression, and prevent joint damage. A healthcare provider can recommend treatments tailored to the specific type and severity of arthritis.
