# AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome)

AIDS is the most advanced stage of infection with the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), a virus that attacks the body's immune system, specifically CD4 cells. Over time, HIV destroys these cells, weakening the body's ability to fight infections and certain cancers. Without treatment, HIV can progress to AIDS, which is defined by a severely weakened immune system and the presence of specific opportunistic infections or cancers. HIV is transmitted through certain bodily fluids, including blood, semen, and breast milk.

Early symptoms of HIV may resemble those of many other illnesses and include fever, fatigue, sore throat, swollen lymph nodes, and a skin rash. These symptoms, known as acute retroviral syndrome, typically appear within weeks of infection. Without treatment, the virus continues to replicate silently, gradually depleting immune cells. As the immune system weakens, individuals become more susceptible to a wide range of infections and illnesses that a healthy immune system would normally control. Advanced symptoms may include persistent fevers, chronic diarrhea, unexplained weight loss, and recurrent infections.

General self-care guidance emphasizes prevention through safe practices, including using barrier methods during sexual activity and avoiding sharing needles or personal items that may be contaminated with blood. Regular health check-ups are important for early detection. If diagnosed with HIV, adhering to antiretroviral therapy is critical for suppressing the virus, maintaining immune function, and reducing the risk of transmission. A balanced diet, regular exercise, and mental health support also play important roles in managing overall well-being.

You should seek medical attention if you believe you have been exposed to HIV, if you experience symptoms consistent with acute HIV infection, or if you are living with HIV and notice new or worsening symptoms. Early diagnosis and consistent treatment significantly improve outcomes and quality of life. HIV testing is widely available, confidential, and recommended as part of routine healthcare.
