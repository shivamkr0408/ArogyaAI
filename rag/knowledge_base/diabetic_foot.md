# Diabetic Foot

Diabetic foot refers to a range of foot problems that can affect individuals with diabetes, primarily resulting from nerve damage (neuropathy) and poor blood flow (peripheral artery disease) in the lower extremities. High blood sugar levels over time can damage nerves, reducing sensation in the feet, and can impair circulation, slowing the healing of cuts and sores. These conditions make the feet highly susceptible to injuries, infections, and ulcers that may not be noticed or may heal poorly. Diabetic foot complications are a leading cause of hospitalization and amputation among people with diabetes, making prevention and early detection critical.

Typical symptoms include numbness or tingling in the feet, reduced ability to feel pain, temperature changes, or pressure, dry and cracked skin, changes in foot shape, slow-healing sores or cuts, blisters, calluses, and in more advanced cases, signs of infection such as redness, swelling, warmth, drainage, or foul odor. Because neuropathy can mask pain, injuries may go unnoticed until they become serious. Poor circulation can further complicate healing and increase the risk of infection spreading.

General self-care guidance is essential for preventing diabetic foot complications. Daily foot inspections to check for cuts, blisters, or changes in skin are important, as is wearing properly fitting, comfortable shoes and socks. Keeping the feet clean and moisturized (avoiding the area between the toes), trimming toenails carefully, and avoiding walking barefoot reduce the risk of injury. Managing blood sugar levels effectively supports nerve and blood vessel health. Regular foot examinations by a healthcare provider are recommended.

You should see a doctor immediately if you notice any open sores, signs of infection, non-healing wounds, or changes in the color or temperature of your feet. Early medical intervention can prevent minor issues from becoming serious complications. Individuals with diabetes should have regular foot assessments as part of their overall diabetes care to catch problems early and maintain foot health.
