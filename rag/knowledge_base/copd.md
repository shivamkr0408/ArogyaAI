# COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)

COPD is a progressive lung disease characterized by long-term breathing problems and poor airflow. It encompasses conditions such as chronic bronchitis and emphysema, which often coexist. The primary cause is long-term exposure to lung irritants, most commonly cigarette smoke, though occupational dust, chemical fumes, and indoor air pollution also contribute. The disease involves ongoing inflammation that damages the airways and air sacs in the lungs, reducing their ability to exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide efficiently. COPD is a leading cause of death and disability worldwide, though early diagnosis and management can significantly improve quality of life.

Typical symptoms include chronic cough, increased mucus production, shortness of breath (especially during physical activity), wheezing, and chest tightness. Symptoms develop gradually and are often dismissed as normal aging or a consequence of smoking, leading to delayed diagnosis. As the disease progresses, breathlessness can occur even at rest, and individuals may experience frequent respiratory infections, fatigue, and unintended weight loss. Exacerbations, or flare-ups, can cause sudden worsening of symptoms and may require hospitalization.

General self-care guidance includes quitting smoking, which is the most important step to slow disease progression. Avoiding lung irritants, practicing breathing exercises, and engaging in regular, appropriate physical activity can help maintain lung function and overall fitness. Annual influenza and pneumococcal vaccinations reduce the risk of respiratory infections. Maintaining a healthy weight, staying hydrated, and managing comorbidities such as heart disease and diabetes support overall health.

You should see a doctor if you experience persistent cough, increased mucus, or shortness of breath, especially if you are a current or former smoker. Early diagnosis through spirometry and other tests allows for timely intervention. If symptoms worsen suddenly or you develop fever, increased sputum, or difficulty breathing at rest, seek prompt medical attention, as these may indicate an exacerbation requiring urgent treatment.
