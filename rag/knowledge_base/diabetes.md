# Diabetes

Diabetes is a chronic metabolic condition characterized by elevated blood sugar levels, resulting from the body's inability to produce or effectively use insulin. There are several types, with Type 1 diabetes involving an autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing cells, and Type 2 diabetes, the most common form, involving insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency. Gestational diabetes occurs during pregnancy and usually resolves after delivery, though it increases the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes later in life. Factors such as genetics, obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and poor diet contribute to the development of Type 2 diabetes.

Typical symptoms include increased thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, and slow-healing wounds. In Type 1 diabetes, symptoms often develop rapidly and can be severe. In Type 2 diabetes, symptoms may develop gradually and can go unnoticed for years, leading to delayed diagnosis. Some individuals may experience tingling or numbness in the hands and feet, dry skin, or recurrent infections. Blood sugar levels can fluctuate, and prolonged elevation can damage organs over time.

General self-care guidance includes maintaining a balanced diet rich in whole grains, lean proteins, vegetables, and healthy fats, while limiting refined sugars and processed foods. Regular physical activity helps improve insulin sensitivity and supports healthy weight management. Monitoring blood sugar levels as recommended, taking prescribed medications consistently, and managing stress are key components of diabetes care. Foot care is particularly important, as poor circulation and nerve damage can increase the risk of complications.

You should see a doctor if you experience symptoms of diabetes, if your blood sugar levels are consistently above or below target ranges, or if you develop complications such as foot ulcers, vision changes, or kidney problems. Regular check-ups with a healthcare provider are essential for monitoring and adjusting your management plan to prevent long-term complications.
